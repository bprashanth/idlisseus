    Here's what the available data shows.
    
    
    
    Cave systems within ~50 km of Krishnagiri
    
    The short answer is: there are no documented limestone karst caves within ~50 km of Krishnagiri that have been surveyed for troglobitic spiders. The area is underlain by granite/gneiss (granetoid gneissic terrain — Krishnagiri's famous black granite), not karst-forming limestone. What exists are granite boulder rock shelters — Neolithic/archaeological sites formed by interlocking boulders on castellated granite hills — rather than true solution caves with the deep, dark, stable-humidity zones that harbor troglobites.
    
    The known troglobitic spider research in the Eastern Ghats is concentrated in the Northern Eastern Ghats (Chhattisgarh), ~700 km from Krishnagiri:
    - Kotumsar Cave (~19.0°N, 81.9°E) — biologically the best-studied cave in India (Biswas 2010)
    - Dandak Cave — a mammalian-dominated cave ecosystem (Biswas & Shrotriya 2011)
    - Mandhip Khol Cave Complex — recent bioinventory
    
    These are well-developed limestone karst systems in the Kanger Valley / Bastar region, a fundamentally different geology from the granitic Southern Eastern Ghats around Krishnagiri.

    Species: Heteropoda venatoria
    Status: Troglophile

    Evidence: Common in cave twilight zones and entrance passages throughout

    ────────────────────────────────────────

    Status: Troglobite (Azores)
    Evidence: The Linyphiidae family has cave-specialist species, but these

    ────────────────────────────────────────
    Species: Mygalomorphs (Idiops etc.)
    Status: Accidental/edaphobitic
    Evidence: Not cavernicolous; occasionally found near entrances but lack
      troglomorphisms
    
    No obligate troglobitic spiders have been described from the Southern Eastern Ghats. Moreau et al. (2023) note that the Deccan limestone caves (Northern Eastern Ghats, not Southern) yielded 18 candidate new troglobitic species, representing a 43% increase in known peninsular Indian troglobitic fauna.
    
    
    
    Abiotic variables known to predict occupancy
    
    From the Kotumsar Cave studies (Biswas 2010 — 25 years of monitoring; Laskar et al. 2021):
    
    1. Humidity — the strongest predictor. Dark-zone humidity >90% (near saturation). Troglobites are restricted to zones where RH never drops below 85%. The granite rock shelters near Krishnagiri are far drier and more variable.
    
    2. Temperature stability — Kotumsar cave maintains 26–28°C year-round (amplitude <2°C). Surface granite shelters in the Krishnagiri area fluctuate far more (day/night and seasonal).
    
    3. Guano presence — bat guano is the primary energy input supporting detritivore communities (Collembola, Diplopoda, cockroaches), which form the prey base for arachnid predators. Kotumsar and Dandak have extensive bat colonies. Without a significant bat roost, the food web collapses.
    
    4. Light gradient (distance from entrance) — spiders show zonation: entrance zone (Heteropoda, Pholcidae), twilight zone (some Linyphiidae), dark zone (true troglobites). Pholcidae occupy the entrance/twilight zone 0–30 m from the entrance.
    
    5. Structural complexity — crevice density, ceiling roughness, and availability of web-anchor points predict pholcid and linyphiid density.
    
    The honest limit: All these variable-occupancy relationships come from Kotumsar/Dandak (Northern Eastern Ghats limestone karst). No equivalent study exists for the granitic rock shelters of the Southern Eastern Ghats near Krishnagiri. The spider occurrence data we have (34 H. venatoria, 75 Pholcidae) lacks microhabitat covariates — no humidity, temperature, or guano measurements are attached to the GBIF/iNat records.
