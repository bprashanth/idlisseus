    WHAT THE PUBLISHED DATA SHOWS
    
    Ahaetulla nasuta complex:
    - Mallik et al. 2020 (Zootaxa 4874) — "Disentangling vines" — is the key reference. They used a multi-criteria approach (genetics + morphology + geography) across Peninsular India. The A. nasuta complex was split into multiple cryptic species, with four new species described from "several latitudinal blocks of the Western Ghats" plus nomenclatural emendations to "plains populations in the Indian peninsula." Critically: their sampling and described splits are organized around the WESTERN GHATS latitudinal gradient and peninsular plains. They do NOT present a dedicated Eastern Ghats vs. Western Ghats phylogeographic comparison for A. nasuta sensu stricto using paired COI + RAG1.
    - Mallik et al. 2019 (PLoS ONE) — described Proahaetulla antiqua from Agasthyamalai (southern Western Ghats), sister to all Ahaetulla, using mtDNA. Deep Oligocene divergence. Again, Western Ghats–centric.
    - Mohapatra et al. 2017 (Zootaxa) — resurrected A. nasuta anomala as A. anomala from ODISHA (i.e., Eastern Ghats / Eastern coastal), using molecular + morphological data. This is the one study that explicitly treats an Eastern Ghats population of the A. nasuta complex — and it was found to be a distinct, valid species (sexually dichromatic: males green, females brown), genetically divergent from both A. nasuta and A. pulverulenta. But the markers and explicit EG-vs-WG sister-relationship framing you describe (COI + RAG1, ranked against WG and peninsular lineages) are not the framing of that paper.
    
    Coelognathus helena:
    - There is essentially NO published molecular phylogeographic study of Coelognathus helena. The five PubMed-indexed papers touching Coelognathus concern C. radiatus nomenclature (David et al. 2023), a coccidian parasite (with C. radiatus/C. flavolineatus as hosts), ticks on Timor-Leste reptiles, a neurotoxin paper (C. radiatus), and a clinical snakebite cohort. None addresses C. helena phylogenetics, let alone with COI + RAG1 across Eastern vs. Western Ghats.
    - Figueroa et al. 2016 (PLoS ONE) — the large species-level snake phylogeny (1745 taxa, 10 loci including RAG1 and mitochondrial genes) — includes colubrids at the species level, but this is a backbone phylogeny, not a within-species phylogeographic comparison of Eastern vs. Western Ghats C. helena populations.
